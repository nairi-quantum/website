Our mission

Securing tomorrow's communication — and keeping Armenian talent home.

Quantum computers will one day break today's encryption, and adversaries already harvest encrypted data to decrypt later. Quantum Secure Direct Communication (QSDC) answers this by sending the message itself through a quantum channel, protected by the laws of physics rather than the difficulty of a calculation.

Nairi Quantum is the first laboratory in Armenia and the region to pursue QSDC. Founded by Dr. Michel Kulhandjian (Rice University), we bring together a team of young Armenian scientists and start from the classical communications and coding expertise where they are strongest — advancing, stage by stage, toward true photonic quantum links.

Our mission is twofold: to help secure the communication of the future, and to give a new generation of Armenian scientists a reason to build advanced careers at home rather than abroad.

Research

What we work on.

Our work spans the path from classical communications to physical quantum links. A few of the directions the team is actively pursuing:

🔑

Quantum Secure Direct Communication

Sending the confidential message itself over a quantum channel — no key to distribute, security from the laws of physics.

📶

Classical-channel realism for QSDC

How real, imperfect coordination channels — loss, delay, jitter, corruption — affect QSDC reliability, latency, and security.

📡

Physical-layer security on SDR

Quantum-inspired emulation on GNU Radio / USRP software-defined radios (classical hardware): waveform masking and eavesdropping detection.

🧮

Quantum & classical coding

Error correction and channel coding — quantum error correction, qudit and stabilizer codes, and modern decoders — with our collaborators.

🌌

Channels: loss, fading & capacity

Link budgets, fading, Shannon and secrecy capacity, and the loss limits that bound quantum communication.

🔬

Toward a photonic testbed

Continuous- versus discrete-variable receivers, and the roadmap to a single-photon quantum link.
